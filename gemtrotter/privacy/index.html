Gemtrotter Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 6, 2026

Gemtrotter ("the game") is made by Ishar Ghura, an independent developer ("I", "me"). This policy explains what data the game and its partners collect, why, and the choices you have. The short version:

  • No accounts. You never sign up, and I never ask for your name, email, phone number, contacts, photos or precise location.
  • Your progress stays on your device. Saves are stored in the game's private app storage.
  • Ads and analytics use device identifiers, and only in the ways you agree to in the consent prompts.
  • Purchases are handled by Apple or Google. I never see your card details.

What is collected, and by whom

1. The game itself (on your device)

DataWhere it livesWhy

I don't run a game server, so none of this is sent to me. If you uninstall the game, it is deleted (unless your phone's own backup copies app data, which is controlled by Apple or Google, not me).

2. Advertising: Google AdMob and its mediation partners

The game shows ads through Google AdMob, which may fill ads from these partners: AppLovin, Unity Ads and Liftoff (Vungle). When an ad is requested or shown, these companies may collect:

  • your device's advertising ID (Android Advertising ID or Apple IDFA, only if you allow it) and other device identifiers such as IDFV or an app-set ID;
  • IP address, from which an approximate location (country or city) can be inferred;
  • device and app information (model, OS version, language, app version);
  • ad interactions (which ads were shown, tapped or watched) and diagnostic data (crashes, load times).

Why: to show ads, cap how often you see them, measure them, prevent fraud and, if you consent, personalize them. These companies process this data under their own privacy policies (links below).

3. Analytics and remote settings: PostHog

The game uses PostHog to understand how the game is played and to tune it. It only starts after you consent. It records:

  • a random install ID created by the game (not your advertising ID, and not linked to your name or account);
  • gameplay events such as starting or finishing a run, the floor reached, watching a rewarded ad and buying an item (product name and price, not payment details);
  • basic device and app information (OS and app version) and the IP address the request comes from, which PostHog may use to estimate your country.

PostHog is also used to deliver remote settings (for example game balance numbers or which version of a shop offer to show). This sends the random install ID so you get a consistent experience.

Why: to fix problems, balance difficulty and decide what to build next. Analytics data is never sold and is not used for advertising.

4. Purchases: Apple App Store and Google Play

In-app purchases are processed entirely by Apple (StoreKit) or Google (Google Play Billing). They handle your payment details under their own policies. The game only receives a confirmation and receipt for the item bought, which it checks on your device to unlock the item.

What is not collected

No name, email, phone number, precise (GPS) location, contacts, photos, microphone, camera, chat or messages. The game has no chat, no user profiles and no social features. There are no loot boxes or other paid random rewards.

Your choices and consent

Consent prompts

  • Ad consent (Google UMP). Where required by law (for example in the EEA, UK and Switzerland), the game shows a consent form from Google before any ads are requested. You can accept, reject, or choose per purpose.
  • Analytics consent. On first launch the game asks whether you want to share anonymous gameplay stats. Nothing is sent to PostHog unless you say yes.
  • App Tracking Transparency (iOS). On iPhone and iPad, iOS asks whether the game may "track" you. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, the IDFA is not shared and ads are not personalized based on your activity in other apps.

Changing your mind

  • In the game: open Settings → Privacy. The Share anonymous stats switch turns analytics on or off at any time. Where the ad consent form is required, an Ad privacy options button reopens it so you can change or withdraw ad consent. Withdrawing stops analytics or personalized ads from then on.
  • On iPhone/iPad: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, then turn off Gemtrotter (or "Allow Apps to Request to Track").
  • On Android: Settings → Privacy → Ads (or Google → Ads), then Delete advertising ID or reset it.
  • Opting out of all ads: reject consent to see only non-personalized ads. The game never shows ads you did not choose to watch.

Even without consent, ads may still be shown. They will be non-personalized, but the ad networks still receive limited data (such as IP address and device type) to deliver the ad, count it and prevent fraud.

Children

Gemtrotter is rated for players about 9–10 and up, but it is a general-audience game and is not directed at children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your country). I do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. Ads are restricted to content suitable for a general audience.

If you are a parent and believe your child has provided personal data through the game, contact me (below) and I will help delete it.

Deleting your data

  • Local data: uninstall the game, or clear its app data (Android: Settings → Apps → Gemtrotter → Storage → Clear data).
  • Analytics data: email me with the subject "Delete my data". Because there are no accounts, I'll need your install ID, shown in Settings → Privacy in the game. I'll delete the matching analytics data within 30 days.
  • Ad data: reset or delete your advertising ID (see above) and use the partners' own tools linked below.

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR, UK GDPR, Canada's PIPEDA, Australia's Privacy Act, the Philippines' Data Privacy Act or US state laws such as the CCPA), you may have rights to access, correct, delete or port your data, to object to processing, and to complain to your local data protection authority. Email me to use any of them. I do not sell personal information. Where sharing data with ad partners counts as "sharing" for targeted advertising under US state laws, you can opt out using the consent and device settings above.

Where data is processed

The developer and some partners are located in, or use servers in, countries other than yours, including the United States. Those partners use legal safeguards such as Standard Contractual Clauses for international transfers.

Security

Data sent by the game to its partners travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Save data stays in the game's private app storage, which other apps can't read.
